Autonomous Vehicles & Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems (ADAS) | Longitudinal Control
  • What It Does: Built a live 2D animated driving simulator in Python where two cars move on screen. The following car automatically adjusts its speed to stay at a safe distance behind a lead car, just like cruise control on a real highway.
  • The Problem: Standard cruise control systems drive at a fixed speed; they cannot slow down when a car suddenly cuts in front or when traffic slows. That gap in safety logic causes rear-end crashes.
  • Adaptive Gap Logic (ACC Brain): Programmed a speed-dependent following gap rule: the faster the car travels, the larger the required safety gap in front. The control algorithm constantly recalculates this gap every frame and adjusts throttle accordingly.
  • Time-To-Collision (TTC) Emergency Braking: When the gap shrank to a danger threshold, the system calculated Time-To-Collision (TTC), which is the number of seconds until the cars would hit, and triggered Autonomous Emergency Braking (AEB). You can literally watch the car smoothly decelerate and stop on screen, frame by frame.
  • Live Telemetry Dashboard: Built a real-time on-screen dashboard inside the Pygame window displaying live numbers for both cars' speeds, the gap distance between them, and current braking status, so you can read exactly what the system is doing at any moment.
  • Functions / Tools: Python, Pygame, Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems (ADAS) Principles, Mathematical Modeling, Time-To-Collision (TTC) Logic, Collision Avoidance.
